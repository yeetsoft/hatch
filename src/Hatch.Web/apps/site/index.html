Hatch manages your AI coding team for you

A kanban board worked by agents as much as by people. You write the tickets, answer the questions and merge the pull requests; an agent takes the rest, one ticket at a time, all night if you let it.

Why Hatch

  • You write the ticket. Hatch runs the team. Say what you want and what done looks like; the board breaks it down, works it, and brings back a pull request.
  • A board, not a chat. Work is cards with keys, columns, comments and an audit trail, so what an agent did is exactly where a person would look for it in six months.
  • Deterministic where it can be, agentic where it must be. Hatch itself decides what is next, who holds what, whether a branch is current and a build is green — in code, the same way every time. A session is spent only on the part that needs judgement: one ticket, one increment, then hand it back.
  • People keep the gates. You decide what is specified, what ships and what is parked. Agents work the columns in between, and ask rather than guess when a decision is not theirs.
  • Decades of software management, written down. Small increments, WIP limits, dependencies that say what lands in order, questions with pressable answers, and a work log beside what each session cost.
  • Yours to run. One line brings the board up on your own machine, under your own domain, with your own data. Nothing in Hatch is true of one installation.
